Part 1: Privacy Policy

This policy applies to every version of the app. Versions differ in one way: some include the Local games tab and some do not. A version without it sends us nothing, and the only data that ever leaves your phone is the news request described below. A version with it sends us only what the Local games section describes, and only when you use that tab.

What happens on your phone: Odds, camera, Tournaments

The Odds tab

Equity, outs, the nuts and pot odds are all worked out on your phone. Nothing you enter there is stored after you leave the app, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Camera mode

If you switch on camera mode in the Odds tab, the app asks for camera access the first time, then uses your phone’s back camera to read the community cards (the flop, turn and river) on the table in front of it and fill them in. It never reads your own cards: you enter those yourself.

  • Each picture is analysed on your phone the moment it is taken and then discarded. No picture or video is ever saved, shown to anyone else or sent anywhere, and nothing the camera reads leaves your phone.
  • The camera runs only while camera mode is on and the Odds tab (or a host’s Table stream page, see below) is open. It stops when you switch the mode off, go to another tab or leave the app.
  • You can withdraw camera access at any time in your phone’s settings. Everything else in the app works without it.

The Tournaments tab

  • Festival list. It is part of the app. Each entry links to the organiser’s official page, which opens in your browser only when you tap it.
  • News. When you open the tab (at most once every 30 minutes, or whenever you tap refresh), your phone downloads the news feeds of the sites in your source list. By default these are PokerNews, Somuchpoker, CardPlayer, PokerTube and Pokerfuse; you can remove any of them and add others. The app sends those sites nothing but the request itself. Like any website you visit, they see your IP address and the app’s browser identifier, under their own privacy policies, which we do not control.
  • The headlines are kept on your phone so the tab still works offline; tapping one opens the story in your browser. Your source list stays on your phone.

Table stream

A host of a Local games room can stream their game to their own Twitch or YouTube channel from the app. What is streamed is a drawing made on the phone: a table with numbered seats (or nicknames the host types), the community cards, the bets and the pot, and a computer voice reading the action. No picture from the camera, no sound from the microphone and no player’s name, face or voice is ever in it. A player’s cards appear only when the host marks them as turned face up at the table. The stream can be delayed by three minutes.

  • The stream goes from the phone straight to Twitch or YouTube under the host’s own account and their terms; we receive nothing of it. The host’s stream key stays in the app’s private storage on the phone and is used only to send the stream.
  • The camera, if the host switches it on, reads only the community cards on the table, as in the Odds tab. The voice is the phone’s own text-to-speech.
  • The same drawn table can also be shown to other users of the app through Watch, if the host switches that on.

Local games: what we collect and why

Local games lets players in the same country find private, no-profit poker games, lets hosts list one, and lets anyone watch a table a host chooses to show. It is the only part of the app that sends anything to us. It works through a small service we run (“the games list”): a Google Apps Script web app and a Google Sheet in our Google account (see Where the list runs). Everything below is sent over an encrypted connection (HTTPS), only when you use the Local games tab, and only for the purpose of running that tab. We collect nothing for advertising, profiling or sale.

Your identity in the app

There is no account and no sign-in. The first time your phone uses Local games it makes up a random identifier and a random secret, keeps them in the app’s private storage, and uses them to mark the rooms, requests and reports you make as yours. The games list stores the identifier and a one-way hash of the secret, never the secret itself. The identifier is not linked to your name, phone number, email, Google account or device identifiers, and we have no way to connect it to you unless you tell us it is yours. Uninstalling the app, or using Delete my data, forgets it; a new installation is a new identity.

Who sees what in Local games

  • Anyone who lists games in that country sees, for each listed game: the host’s chosen name, the area (never an address), the date and time, the type of game, blinds, seats and how many are taken or on the reserve list, the entry, the note, whether joining takes a code, and the host’s stream link if given.
  • The host of a game sees the chosen name and message of each player who asks to join, and their request’s status.
  • A player the host accepts sees the host’s contact handle while the game stays listed, and any reply the host wrote to them.
  • Only the host sees the invitation code. It never appears in a listing. A player who enters it is told only whether it was right.
  • Anyone who opens Watch sees, for a table being shown, the game’s listing, how many are watching, the drawn table and its commentary line, with the seat labels the host typed.
  • We (the developer) can read everything in the games list’s sheet, which is how reports are reviewed and games taken down.
  • Nobody else. We do not sell, rent, trade or share any of it, and we show it to no advertiser, data broker or analytics service.

Watch

Watch shows tables that hosts have chosen to share. A table is shown after the same delay the host set for the stream (none, or three minutes), and only while the host keeps sharing it. The games list keeps a shared table in a short-lived cache for two minutes after the host’s last update and never writes it to the sheet. While you watch, your identifier is kept in that cache for 90 seconds to count viewers, and nothing else about you is recorded. Hosts see the count, not who is watching. If a host gave a Twitch or YouTube link, the Watch page offers to open it in your browser; what those sites do is governed by their policies.

Where hosting is on: the country check

Hosting and joining games are available only in countries on a list built into the app. To decide which list applies, the app reads the country of your SIM card and of the mobile network you are on (or, with neither, your phone’s language setting). This is read on your phone, needs no permission, uses no location service and is never sent anywhere. If any country reported is not on the list, the Local games tab offers Watch only.

How long we keep it, and how to delete it

  • A listed game stays visible until six hours after its start time, or until the host closes it or we take it down. Requests stay with their game.
  • The rows for games, requests and reports stay in the games list’s sheet after that. We keep them to enforce the posting limits and to deal with abuse. We may delete them at any time.
  • Delete my data, in the profile page of the Local games tab, erases your name, contact, notes, messages and report reasons from every row you made, erases the replies you wrote as a host, closes your open games, withdraws your requests and takes down any table you were showing; then the app forgets your identity. What is left of your rows holds no name, contact or text: only timestamps and identifiers, kept for the limits. Deletion needs a network connection and happens at once.
  • Shared tables are gone two minutes after the host’s last update, at once when the host stops sharing, when the game closes, or when the host uses Delete my data. Viewer counts are gone 90 seconds after your last view.
  • If you cannot use the app (for example you no longer have the phone), write to us (see Contact) with the name and contact handle you used, and we will erase what we can find.

Where the list runs: Google

The games list is a Google Apps Script web app and a Google Sheet in our Google account. Google stores and processes the data on our behalf, on its own servers, which may be outside the country you are in (including outside Singapore and the European Economic Area), under Google’s terms for its services and Google’s privacy policy (policies.google.com/privacy). As with any web request, Google’s infrastructure sees your IP address and request details and may keep its own technical logs under its policies; the games list itself does not receive or store IP addresses. Where data protection law requires it, we rely on Google’s contractual commitments for transfers outside your country. We use no other service provider, and no other third party receives Local games data.

Security

Every exchange with the games list is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Your secret never leaves your phone in a form the list keeps: the list stores a one-way hash of it, and a request is honoured only when the hash matches. Rooms show an area, never an address; a host’s contact reaches only the players that host accepts. Text you enter is cut to length and stored so that it can never run as a formula or script. Access to the sheet is limited to our Google account. No system is perfectly secure, and the app is offered without a guarantee of security; if we learn of a breach affecting your data we will act, and notify you and the authorities, as the law requires.
